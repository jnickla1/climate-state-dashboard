How to read this dashboard

Historical benchmark. Crossing years use the realized observational centered 20-year mean. Its rate uses centered 10-year means around the crossing. These blue benchmarks also appear on anthropogenic views as an observational comparison. A centered 20-year benchmark is available through 2015.

Seven late crossings. The preindustrial 1.0°C benchmark includes 93 observed crossings and seven estimates from quartic fits to the last 25 available centered 10-year means. These estimates are identified in the downloads; their model uncertainty is not included in the reported ranges.

Method comparisons. Red segments extend 15 years backward from the selected method’s mean first crossing. Their slopes are endpoint derivatives of quadratic fits to 15 past annual smoothed outputs. Crossing ranges condition on the members that cross.

2025 estimates. Levels use current method outputs. Rates use corrected retrospective outputs for 2011–2025; GWI uses its current outputs as an explicit temporary retrospective proxy. The red 15-year line is anchored at the current level and fitted rate. Level ranges combine the members’ calibrated Gaussian distributions; rate ranges include fit and member variability.

Probabilities. The three assessment windows use calibrated current probabilities already saved by the paper pipeline. Outside those windows, and for the fourth threshold, the display uses Gaussian approximations with the same calibrated means and uncertainties. The bottom curves use the paper’s cumulative-maximum and one-year Gaussian smoothing convention; they are density proxies, not a joint first-passage model.

Satellite calibration. Values are anomalies relative to 1981–2010. The reference thresholds are shifted by 0.7°C, following the source pipeline. Both the calibration and its threshold labels change together.

Background lines. The Background lines menu switches only the grey/black curves between raw heads/tails observations and the selected method’s smoothed ensemble outputs. Numerical estimates, benchmarks, and probabilities do not change.

Distribution scales. All three panels default to 0–0.3 probability/year. Bars exceeding the ceiling have // labels with their actual heights; hover values retain the full fraction. The magnify switch gives the rightmost threshold an explicitly labeled finer y-axis and does not renormalize its probabilities.

Browser interaction. Charts use the exported Python results, with zooming and a data cursor in the browser. Python calculations run locally; they are not rerun by the browser.
